# Assessment

Assessment is based on a mixture of practice-based and reflection-based work. You are in control of how much you do (a perspective based in specification-based and contract-based grading pedagogical approaches). I do this because I want you to **explore** and experiment. To push yourself out of your comfort level. I do not want you trying to game the system to figure out the secret curriculum and what I'm 'really' after.

{: .note } 
I want you to explore, to experiment, and to try things out. The more things you try, in good-faith, the higher your grade is. Do more things = higher grade. The 'good-faith' bit is important. Using chatGPT to write something for you is not good-faith.  The last version of this class was ruined by people submitting computer-generated text as if it was their own. 

There are two kinds of assessment pieces in this course.

## Practice-based exercises

Practice-based - these are exercises with no right or wrong answer. They are opportunities for you to learn a variety of digital skills. They therefore require good-faith effort and honest reckoning of what went wrong and what went right. Right? I'm not trying to catch you out and say 'ah ha! you have failed the exercise! D- for you!'. Everyone comes to digital/technical work with differing levels of previous exposure, comfort, knowledge. A good-faith effort for someone who knows a bit of python will look different from a good-faith effort for someone who is a wizard with video-editing. Both have something useful to contribute to digital archaeology. Therefore, for practice-based exercises I require the following:

A weekly compendium containing:
- A log of what you tried and what happened, kept in Github.
- any files you create in the course of trying an exercise, kept in Github
- an overview note connecting what you tried to what you've read, what I've said in class, and/or other exercises you've tried

That's it. A 'compendium' can be a subfolder in your github class repository.

10 weeks.
